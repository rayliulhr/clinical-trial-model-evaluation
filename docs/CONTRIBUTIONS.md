# Contributions and authorship

## Hongrui Liu's role

Hongrui Liu is a Biomedical Sciences PhD candidate at Johns Hopkins University. His contribution represented here is scientific problem formulation and evaluation planning at the interface of biomedical research and computational modeling.

He initiated and directed an evidence-first review of a collaborative patient-model research effort: asking that code, inputs, recorded executions, and reported conclusions be checked separately; requesting explicit task definitions and fair comparator designs; and prioritizing what evidence should be collected next. He contributed to the research questions and team discussions that motivated this portfolio.

The portfolio presents the resulting evaluation approach as general, independently authored educational material. It does not assign sole credit for the broader team's model architecture, code, data preparation, statistical advice, or experiments to Hongrui.

## AI assistance

AI tools assisted with local source inspection, synthesis, drafting, writing this new demonstration code, and automated checks. The code should be described as AI-assisted development directed by Hongrui, rather than as evidence of unaided software authorship. Review logs distinguish automated checks from human review; preparation does not imply statistical-advisor approval.

## What this work demonstrates

- Translating a biomedical question into a population, prediction time, information set, endpoint, and evaluation plan.
- Asking for evidence that separates reproducibility, model performance, and clinical usefulness.
- Identifying sources of unfair comparison and proposing controlled experiments.
- Working with AI tools to create inspectable research artifacts and test their calculations.

The synthetic demo is new work for this portfolio. Neither its numerical outputs nor its implementation are taken from the collaborative project. All proposed experiments are labeled as proposals.
